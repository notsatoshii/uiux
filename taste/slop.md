# Slop: the tells

Each item below, present on the surface without a reason written in the brief, is an automatic finding (severity 2 on a persuade surface, 3 elsewhere). Three or more on one screen cap the aesthetic score at 5.

## Layout

- Centred hero: headline, one line of sub, two buttons (one filled, one outlined), a floating product screenshot with a soft shadow.
- Three equal cards in a row, each with an icon in a tinted circle, a bold title and two lines of text. Six of them in a grid is the same thing twice.
- "Trusted by" logo strip with grey logos nobody checked.
- Alternating left/right feature rows, image + text, four times.
- A testimonial carousel with stock avatars and five stars.
- A pricing table with a "Most popular" badge on the middle column, placed by habit rather than by data.
- A final CTA band that repeats the hero's headline.
- Everything inside a max-width container with equal padding, so every section has the same height and density.

## Colour and surface

- Cream background (#FDFBF7 and friends) with an arbitrary serif display heading on a tech product.
- Purple-to-blue gradient; gradient text on the headline; gradient blobs floating behind the hero.
- Glassmorphism on a light background with nothing behind the glass.
- Grey shadows (`rgba(0,0,0,.1)`) under everything, at the same size everywhere.
- Rounded-xl (or larger) on every element regardless of size; pills on things that are not pills.
- One accent colour used on every section so it stops meaning anything.
- Dark mode that is pure black with pure white text.

## Type

- Inter (or the system stack) at default weights with no scale decisions; a 48px heading because that is the template.
- Tracking-tight on every heading, including small ones.
- Headlines that start with a verb and a power word: "Unlock", "Supercharge", "Elevate", "Transform", "Empower", "Seamless", "Effortless", "Next-generation", "AI-powered".
- Korean that reads as a translation: 당신, noun stacks, passive strings, idioms carried over, 보장/반드시 promises.
- Emoji as bullets or section markers.
- Body text at 14px on desktop and 16px everywhere else "because mobile".

## Imagery and icons

- Icons from three different sets with three stroke weights.
- Illustrations of flat people with oversized limbs in purple and teal.
- An abstract 3D blob, a glowing orb, or a "neural network" of dots and lines as the hero image.
- Stock photos of a laptop on a desk with a coffee.
- Screenshots with the real UI blurred or with lorem ipsum showing.

## Motion

- Everything fades up on scroll with the same 600 ms ease, including body text.
- Parallax on a hero image for no reason.
- Infinite marquee of logos or words.
- Hover scale on cards that also lifts the shadow, on a touch-first product.
- A loading spinner where a skeleton or the content itself would do.
- Bouncing anything on a product that is not for children.

## Copy and structure

- Feature names that describe the technology, not the user's job.
- A "How it works" in three numbered steps that are not actually how it works.
- Claims with no receipt ("10× faster", "loved by thousands").
- FAQ that answers questions nobody asked to pad the page.
- A chatbot bubble bottom-right on day one.

## Not slop (do not flag)

- A plain page when the brief's words are plain: restraint is a choice when the brief makes it.
- A pattern from this list that the brief asks for by name and explains.
- A convention of the platform on an operate surface (a sidebar, a table, a toolbar): native expectations outrank novelty there.
